About this edition

How this edition works

A verse number is a link wherever John Gill comments on that verse, and opens his comment; the heading above each comment leads back to the verse. The references printed under the section headings are this translation’s own parallel passages, and each leads to its place in the text. The small figures are the translators’ notes, listed below the chapter. Gill’s introduction to each book is on that book’s title page.

Gill expounded the Authorized (King James) Version, so the clauses he quotes, printed in bold in the commentary, are in the words of that version and will sometimes differ from the Berean Standard Bible text printed above them.

Finding your way

Type a reference in the box at the top of any page (“John 3:16”, “ps 23”, “1 cor 13”) and press Enter to go straight there; a single word looks that word up in the concordance. The arrows beside a chapter number, or the left and right arrow keys, move to the previous and next chapter, and the chapter list beside them jumps within the book. Selecting a verse number shows Gill’s comment on that verse in a box over the text, with a link to the full commentary page; selecting a small figure shows its note in place. The commentary pages carry a row of verse numbers for jumping within a chapter. The home page remembers where you last read, and the reading plans mark today’s portion.

Sources

Text. Berean Standard Bible: text, section headings and footnotes from the publisher’s own current files (bereanbible.com). Dedicated to the public domain by its publisher in 2023.

What is left out of the commentary. Where Gill gives a verse no comment of its own and only sends the reader to his comment on another verse, that verse has no entry here (about 1,700 verses, chiefly in Chronicles, Numbers, Exodus, Ezra and Nehemiah).

Commentary. John Gill, An Exposition of the Old Testament (1748–1763) and An Exposition of the New Testament (1746–1748). Public domain. The electronic text descends from the transcription made for the Online Bible. Gill’s footnotes appear as lettered notes after each verse. His Hebrew is printed without vowel points and his Greek without accents, as the transcription has them; in Judges, Jonah, Philemon and a few scattered verses, supplied from a second copy, the Hebrew and Greek words are wanting. That transcription’s modern editor added remarks of his own; those that could be identified have been removed.

Cross-references. The parallel passages under the section headings are those of the Berean Standard Bible itself.

Concordance. Generated from the text of this edition. Common words are left out; the remaining words are ranked by frequency and the most frequent kept. Where a word occurs in more verses than there is room to list, references are chosen book by book so that the selection ranges across the whole Bible, preferring in each book the verse in which the word is most prominent.

Reading plans. M‘Cheyne’s calendar (1842) is in the public domain. The chronological plan was compiled for this edition.

Type. The website is set in EB Garamond (Georg Duffner and Octavio Pardo), used under the SIL Open Font Licence.

Three translations in one place

This website holds the King James Version, the Berean Standard Bible and the Majority Standard Bible. The box at the top left changes translation and keeps your place. “Compare translations”, beside each chapter heading, sets two or three of them verse by verse. Gill’s Exposition is the same under all three, with the verse printed in whichever translation you are reading.
